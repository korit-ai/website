Most professional 3D mapping tools are built indoor-first, for GPS-denied
environments. That's the opposite of how autonomous vehicles are engineered,
where outdoor and indoor-like conditions — tunnels, parking structures,
dense urban canyons — are fused continuously, without a hard seam. We think
mapping hardware should work the same way: one continuous track from the
street to the stairwell.

That's what we're building at korit.ai — a single sensor platform (360°
LiDAR, global-shutter camera, IMU, and GNSS/INS, writing to a hot-swappable
SSD) that moves between four physical mounts — worn, vehicle-mounted,
robot-mounted, or fixed — depending on how a site needs to be captured. Raw
data is processed off-device, so the same core can serve very different
workflows.

We're focused right now on construction documentation, infrastructure and
facility surveys, facility management, and heritage/as-built records — early
conversations with partners, not a finished list. If one of those sounds
like your world, we'd like to hear how you map it today.

korit.ai is in early development: our founder's background is in automotive
SLAM, LiDAR, and GNSS/INS sensor fusion, and our partner's is in civil and
architecture engineering, grounding the hardware in what real sites actually
need.

If you'd like to see your own site mapped, [request a demo](/en/demo/) — no
obligation, and we'll follow up to schedule it.
